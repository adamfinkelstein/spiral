Center: — drag in the left image to move it (hold Shift for 10× finer). Arrow keys nudge 1 px (Shift: 0.1 px). Double-click to recenter.

The fits assume the ring spacing is known: Spacing error makes them use the true spacing × (1 + error), here px, to test how sensitive they are to an imperfect spacing estimate.

Noise sigma adds Gaussian noise (in units of full white) to the chunk image before thresholding, so some line pixels drop out and some background pixels join the components.

Tangential blur (default 3 px; 0 = off): first estimate a rough center from the image's gradient lines (as on the spacing page; green +), then, after the denoising blur, blur the image along circles around that center. That averages noise along the arcs without smearing across them, before thresholding.

Estimation: blur the noisy chunk image a little (Denoise blur), and along the rings (Tangential blur), then threshold it (Threshold) into 8-connected components. (Hysteresis thresholding was used before the tangential blur; with it, a single threshold does as well.) Small components are dropped, and each is grown by 2 px into its soft edges. Each is then fit using grey levels as weights: a circle fit first (Kåsa, then geometric, then its center shifted to correct for the spiral's ≈ b = spacing/2π sideways offset), then center and phase of a spiral with the known spacing (Levenberg–Marquardt on the perpendicular distance to the nearest arm). Unchecking Spiral refinement keeps the circle fit's center.

Shared start: instead of each chunk starting from its own circle fit, (1) the corrected circle fits of chunks reaching Min extent vote, and the median of their x and of their y is the start for every chunk's spiral fit; (2) the median of the kept spiral fits' centers is then the start for a second, final round.

Combine chunks fits one spiral to all kept chunks, RANSAC-style over chunks. Hypotheses are each chunk's own fit and a joint fit of every pair of chunks (sampled if there are more than 20 chunks). A chunk agrees with a hypothesis if its loss with that center and phase is within Agreement τ of its loss under its own fit (Δ in the table): a genuine chunk that slid along its flat valley still fits the true spiral almost as well. The hypothesis whose disagreements cost least (each capped at τ, weighted by pixels) wins; then one center and phase are fitted to all agreeing chunks, repeating until the set of agreeing chunks is stable.

Chunk model: how much the combined spiral is shared. Shared spacing & phase: one perfect spiral for all chunks. Own phase per chunk: only the center is shared; each chunk may sit slightly in or out from the ideal curve. Own phase & spacing: each chunk also has its own spacing, held near the nominal one by Spacing tolerance (long chunks set their own, short ones stay near nominal), as in a real, imperfect spiral; the table's local s column shows it, with its error against the true local spacing in grey.

Robust loss uses Tukey's biweight, reweighting each round: pixels farther from their arm than the Outlier cutoff add a fixed maximum loss but no pull, so they can't drag the fit, and moving the curve off the pixels makes the loss larger, not smaller. Inliers are pixels within half the cutoff of their arm; inl% is their (weighted) fraction, and fits below Min inlier fraction are discarded. Pixels scattered at random would score about the cutoff as a fraction of spacing (50% at 0.5), so this mainly rejects noise blobs.

Each chunk is a piece of the spiral covering a random angle between 0 and Max extent, placed uniformly at random (by arc length) along the whole spiral. Components smaller than Min component pixels are dropped before fitting; fits whose estimated extent is below Min extent (or with too few inliers, or whose center lands within one spacing of the chunk itself) are discarded (grey, no estimate drawn, faded in the table).
