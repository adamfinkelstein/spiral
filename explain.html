Spiral FFT: controls

Source code: github.com/adamfinkelstein/spiral

The left panel shows a white spiral (or set of concentric rings) on black, computed on a 512×512 grid. The right panel shows the 2D Fourier transform of that image: the log of its magnitude, with zero frequency at the center. Both panels show only their right two-thirds, since the images are nearly symmetric; the full image is still drawn and transformed. The blue lines mark the center of each canvas.

Moving the center

Drag in the left image
Moves the center of the spiral (the red dot). The drag is relative to where you click, so you can grab anywhere. Hold Shift while dragging for 10× finer movement.
Arrow keys
Nudge the center by 1 pixel, or 0.1 pixel with Shift.
Double-click
Returns the center to the middle of the canvas.

Distances are in pixels of the 512×512 image. The center can sit at a fraction of a pixel, and the spiral is drawn exactly there.

Shape

Ring spacing (px)
Distance between successive turns of the spiral, or between rings. The spiral follows r = bθ with b = spacing / 2π, so the radius grows by one spacing per turn.
Start radius (px)
Radius where the spiral begins. In rings mode, the smallest ring drawn has at least this radius.
End radius (px)
Radius where the spiral ends. In rings mode, the largest ring drawn has at most this radius.
Rotation (deg)
Rotates the spiral about its center. Has no effect (and is greyed out) in rings mode.
Line width (px)
Width of the line, measured where it is at half brightness.
Rings/spiral
Checked: a spiral. Unchecked: concentric circles at radii that are multiples of the ring spacing, kept only if they fall between the start and end radii.

Line edges

Smooth line edges
Checked: each line edge fades smoothly from white to black, following a Gaussian blur (erf) profile. Unchecked: every pixel is either fully on or fully off, depending on whether its center falls inside a line.
Edge transition (px)
How wide the fade is, from 10% to 90% brightness. Only used with smooth edges.

Why this matters: sharp edges contain frequencies higher than the 512 grid can represent. In the Fourier transform those wrap around and show up as curved arcs coming in from the edges and corners of the right panel (aliasing). A smooth transition of about 2–3 px removes them.

Fourier transform

FFT contrast
Brightness curve for the right panel. Values above 1 brighten faint detail; values below 1 emphasize only the strongest parts. The display is scaled to the brightest value other than the zero-frequency point.
Subtract mean
Subtracts the image's average brightness before transforming, which removes the very bright spot at the center of the spectrum.
Hann window
Fades the image smoothly to black toward its borders before transforming. This suppresses the cross-shaped streaks that appear when the image has content near its edges, because the transform treats the image as repeating.
Show 1/spacing ring
Draws red circles on the spectrum at radius 512 / spacing and its multiples. These are where the energy from evenly spaced rings should land, so you can check the transform against them.

Radial profile plot

The plot below the two panels averages the Fourier transform around circles centered on zero frequency. Every pixel of the right panel is put into a bin by its distance from the center, rounded to the nearest whole number, and the plot shows the average brightness of each bin. Brightness is the log magnitude scaled so the brightest point (other than zero frequency) is 1, which is what the right panel shows at contrast 1; the contrast slider does not affect the plot. Bright rings in the transform show up as peaks. With Show 1/spacing ring on, red dashed lines mark where those peaks are expected. Beyond radius 256 (the shaded part) the circles only pass through the corners of the transform, so those bins average fewer pixels.

The red curve is a harmonic-sum score that turns the repeated peaks into a single peak. Rings spaced s pixels apart produce peaks at radii k·512/s for k = 1, 2, 3, … For each candidate first-peak radius ρ, the score adds up, over all its multiples kρ up to radius 256, how far the profile there rises above the points halfway to the neighbouring multiples, then divides by the square root of the number of terms. Only the true ρ lines up with every peak, so the score has one clear maximum there. The curve is plotted at x = ρ and scaled to fill the plot. The text in the corner gives the maximum's radius and the spacing it implies, 512 / ρ, which should match the ring spacing slider. Candidates cover spacings from about 3 to 64 px.
