Spiral FFT: controls

What each control does. For the method itself, step by step with its parameters and test results, see Procedure. Source code: github.com/adamfinkelstein/spiral

The left panel shows a white spiral (or set of concentric rings) on black, computed on a 512×512 grid. The right panel shows the 2D Fourier transform of that image: the log of its magnitude, with zero frequency at the center. Both panels show only their right two-thirds, since the images are nearly symmetric; the full image is still drawn and transformed. The blue lines mark the center of each canvas.

The Defaults button resets every control to its starting value and moves the center back to the middle.

Moving the center

Drag in the left image
Moves the center of the spiral (the red dot). The drag is relative to where you click, so you can grab anywhere. Hold Shift while dragging for 10× finer movement.
Arrow keys
Nudge the center by 1 pixel, or 0.1 pixel with Shift.
Double-click
Returns the center to the middle of the canvas.

Distances are in pixels of the 512×512 image. The center can sit at a fraction of a pixel, and the spiral is drawn exactly there.

Shape

Ring spacing (px)
Distance between successive turns of the spiral, or between rings. The spiral follows r = bθ with b = spacing / 2π, so the radius grows by one spacing per turn.
Start radius (px)
Radius where the spiral begins. In rings mode, the smallest ring drawn has at least this radius.
End radius (px)
Radius where the spiral ends. In rings mode, the largest ring drawn has at most this radius.
Phase (deg)
Phase of the spiral, i.e. the angle at which its arms cross a given radius; changing it rotates the spiral about its center. Has no effect (and is greyed out) in rings mode.
Offset at 20%, 40%, 60%, 80% (× spacing)
Real spirals are never perfect. These bend the curve away from the ideal r = bθ: the radius is shifted by the given amount (in ring spacings, −1 to 1) at 20%, 40%, 60% and 80% of the way (by θ) from the start to the end radius, by 0 at both ends, and linearly in between, so the curve stays continuous but its slope has kinks. In rings mode each ring is shifted by the offset at its radius. Use them to test how well the spacing estimate holds up when the curve isn't a perfect spiral.
Aspect (x, 1/y)
Scales the whole drawing about its center by the aspect value horizontally (0.95 to 1.05; 1 = no change) and by 1 / aspect vertically, so its area stays the same. Line widths scale too, as if the finished image had been resized. Ring spacing becomes spacing × aspect horizontally and spacing / aspect vertically, so the rings in the Fourier transform become slightly elliptical and the peaks in the radial profile smear. Use it to test how well the spacing estimate holds up when the drawing isn't perfectly circular.
Line width (px)
Width of the line, measured where it is at half brightness. The slider runs from 0 to the current ring spacing (wider lines would merge into a solid disc); lowering the spacing shrinks the width if needed.
Advanced peak search
On (default): the spacing estimate searches only between half and twice the target spacing (here the true spacing, standing in for a device's nominal spacing; dashed red lines on the plot), works coarse to fine down to the expected variation below, and warns when its answer lands on the edge of that range. Off: the original single search for sharp peaks over all spacings. Details: Procedure, step 5.
Expected spacing variation (±%)
How much the ring spacing may vary along the spiral (e.g. the device's tolerance; default 10%). The search starts broad and stops refining at this variation: refining further makes the estimate lock onto one region's spacing instead of the average. At 0 it refines all the way (best for a perfect spiral). Only used with Advanced peak search; the steps are listed under the plot.
Noise sigma
Adds Gaussian noise (standard deviation in units of full white; a fixed random pattern) to the image after it is drawn and blurred, so both the Fourier transform and the gradient-vote center see a noisy image.
Gradient-line center
Shows the spiral's center as estimated from the image alone (used by Turn weighting): lines along the image gradients, intersected by RANSAC and corrected for the spiral, with the image blurred along the rings to resist noise (Procedure, step 1). Green pixels: the lines that agree; green +: the estimate; green circles: earlier rounds. The error against the true center is shown under the controls. Lines per orientation and Strength floor choose which gradient pixels become lines; Tangential blur sets the blur along the rings (0 = none). Show blurred image replaces the left image with the blurred image the final lines came from; the Fourier transform still uses the unblurred image.
Turn weighting
In the Fourier transform every turn counts in proportion to its length, so outer turns dominate ("by length"). "1/r" multiplies the image by 1/r around the gradient-line center before the transform, so every turn counts equally; "1/√r" (the default) is halfway between, and was the best compromise in tests (Procedure, step 2).
Rings/spiral
Checked: a spiral. Unchecked: concentric circles at radii that are multiples of the ring spacing, kept only if they fall between the start and end radii.

Line edges

Smooth line edges
Checked: each line edge fades smoothly from white to black, following a Gaussian blur (erf) profile. Unchecked: every pixel is either fully on or fully off, depending on whether its center falls inside a line.
Edge transition (px)
How wide the fade is, from 10% to 90% brightness. Only used with smooth edges.
Blur sigma (px)
Blurs the finished image with a Gaussian of this standard deviation before the Fourier transform (0 = no blur). The left panel shows the blurred image. Unlike smooth edges, which keep sharp detail from being drawn in the first place, blurring afterwards cannot undo aliasing that is already in the pixels: it dims the whole outer part of the spectrum, real content and aliased arcs alike.

Why this matters: sharp edges contain frequencies higher than the 512 grid can represent. In the Fourier transform those wrap around and show up as curved arcs coming in from the edges and corners of the right panel (aliasing). A smooth transition of about 2–3 px removes them.

Fourier transform

FFT contrast
Brightness curve for the right panel. Values above 1 brighten faint detail; values below 1 emphasize only the strongest parts. The display is scaled to the brightest value other than the zero-frequency point.
Subtract mean
Subtracts the image's average brightness before transforming, which removes the very bright spot at the center of the spectrum.
Hann window
Fades the image smoothly to black toward its borders before transforming. This suppresses the cross-shaped streaks that appear when the image has content near its edges, because the transform treats the image as repeating.
Show 1/spacing
Draws red circles on the spectrum at radius 512 / spacing and its multiples. These are where the energy from evenly spaced rings should land, so you can check the transform against them.

Radial profile plot

The plot below the two panels is drawn against radius ρ in the Fourier transform (FFT bins): rings spaced s px apart put peaks at ρ = 512 / s and its multiples. See Procedure (steps 3–5) for how the estimate is computed.

White curve
The radial profile: mean brightness of the transform (log magnitude, at contrast 1) at each distance from its center. Beyond radius 256 (shaded) the circles only pass through the transform's corners. The contrast slider does not affect it.
Red curve
The harmonic-sum score for each candidate first-peak radius, from the final stage of the search; its highest point (red dot) gives the estimate.
Text
The peak radius and the estimated spacing; with Advanced peak search, the estimate after each coarse-to-fine stage, and a warning if it lands on the edge of the search range.
Purple bar
The estimated spacing on its own 0–64 px scale, labelled in purple across the top.
Dashed red lines
The edges of the search range (×0.5–2 of the target spacing) with Advanced peak search, and the expected harmonics with Show 1/spacing.
